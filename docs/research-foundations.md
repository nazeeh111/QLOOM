# Research foundations and claim boundary

Reviewed 25 October 2026. This is a source-based design assessment, not a claim to have reproduced the cited papers. QLOOM's measured results and installed versions belong in the experiment artifacts.

## What the established tools already do

| Foundation | Established capability | Appropriate role in QLOOM |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Qiskit](https://github.com/Qiskit/qiskit) | Circuit representation, gate decomposition, staged compilation, layout and routing, and several optimization strengths. | Interchange, basic gate lowering, and optimization-level baselines. QLOOM's learned strategy must have its own implementation and measurable effect beyond choosing a transpile level. |
| [Qiskit Aer](https://github.com/Qiskit/qiskit-aer) | CPU circuit simulation, including statevector and density-matrix methods and explicit noise models. | Independent noiseless checks and synthetic-noise evaluation. [Simulator documentation](https://qiskit.github.io/qiskit-aer/stubs/qiskit_aer.AerSimulator.html). |
| [MQT QMAP](https://github.com/munich-quantum-toolkit/qmap) | Exact constraint-solving and heuristic mapping, Clifford synthesis, and hardware-specific compilation. | Strong mapping baseline under the identical connectivity graph. Its exact mapper optimizes inserted SWAP/H operations within its formulation, rather than every possible subsequent resynthesis. [Mapping guide](https://mqt.readthedocs.io/projects/qmap/en/latest/mapping.html). |
| [BQSKit](https://github.com/BQSKit/bqskit) | Numerical synthesis, partitioning, retargeting, placement, routing, and configurable compiler workflows. | A synthesis-oriented baseline with the same physical width, connectivity, and gate set. Report its synthesis tolerance, worker count, and actual wall time. |

The QMAP reference is Robert Wille and Lukas Burgholzer, *MQT QMAP: Efficient Quantum Circuit Mapping*, ISPD 2023, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3569052.3578928). BQSKit's capabilities and standard load/model/compile/export workflow are documented in its linked repository and [project site](https://bqskit.lbl.gov/).

## Requested studies

[Kremer et al., arXiv:2405.13196v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13196v2), *Practical and efficient quantum circuit synthesis and transpiling with Reinforcement Learning* (2024, revised February 2025), already studies learned synthesis and routing. Its method uses PPO and curriculum learning, increasing target difficulty as success improves. Training is specific to an operator class, gate set, and connectivity. The authors report training infrastructure with 128 CPU cores and an A100, with model training ranging from minutes to under 48 hours. Those are their conditions and results, not QLOOM's. This supports using a much smaller measured-evaluation surrogate for a CPU demonstration, while ruling out any claim that learning quantum routing is itself original.

[Wang et al., arXiv:2406.12195](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12195v1), *Quantum Compiling with Reinforcement Learning on a Superconducting Processor* (2024), studies gate-sequence synthesis with a DQN and real superconducting hardware evaluation. The paper reports a 60-million-parameter architecture and billions of training examples for its two- and three-qubit settings. Its three-qubit QFT example finds a seven-CZ implementation. A small CPU surrogate cannot be presented as a replication of this experiment. QLOOM instead fixes a supported program's meaning and searches hardware-compatible implementations; it makes no real-QPU claim.

## Additional methods that constrain originality

- [MQT Predictor / Quetschlich et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04508) already learns compiler pass sequences using passes from Qiskit and TKET. The maintained [Predictor project](https://github.com/munich-quantum-toolkit/predictor) also performs supervised device selection. Learned pass ordering and combining compilers are established ideas.
- [Quarl](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10120) learns circuit rewrites using graph representations and a factorized action space. Its [source](https://github.com/quantum-compiler/Quarl) builds on Quartz's automatically generated and verified circuit transformations. A graph model and rewrite search alone are not a novelty claim.
- [OrQstrator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09682) (2025, extended abstract) proposes orchestration among learned rewriting, local resynthesis, and gate-set translation. Treat its orchestration claim as prior work, rather than claiming an unexplored category.
- [Graph-Based Bayesian Optimization for Quantum Circuit Architecture Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09586) (December 2025, revised July 2026) uses a graph surrogate and acquisition function for variational circuit discovery. Its goal is classifier architecture quality, so its candidates need not be semantically equivalent to one fixed input program. This distinction matters when borrowing search ideas.
- [Q-PreSyn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19738) (January 2026) learns local equivalence-preserving edits before synthesis to reduce T-count. It is relevant to the value of transformation order, but addresses a different gate-cost regime.
- [QBalance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02966) (May 2026) already combines multi-objective strategy selection, a survival-product noise proxy, a Bayesian linear ordering surrogate, and reproducible artifacts. Its paper explicitly states that its bandit orders candidates without reducing their number of evaluations. A real hard evaluation budget and reported reduction against exhaustive exploration are therefore meaningful distinctions for QLOOM, but require measurement.
- [Tao et al., arXiv:2608.19103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19103) (August 2026) embeds deterministic commutation and cancellation within RL. Simple reductions should remain reliable compiler operations; learning should spend its budget on decisions those reductions do not settle.
- [The MQT Compiler Collection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08674) (April 2026) addresses structured quantum-classical programs using MLIR. QLOOM's bounded gate model does not cover that full compiler scope.

These sources are a focused related-work review, not an exhaustive novelty or patent search.

## Defensible QLOOM contribution

The proposed contribution is an inspectable, CPU-compatible **budgeted search system over an independently implemented compiler**, with topology-aware features, real candidate-evaluation training labels, strict logical validation, and reproducible artifact export. Its hypotheses are that circuit/device graph information improves candidate selection, and that a compact surrogate can find useful implementations with fewer evaluations than exhaustive exploration. These are experimental questions. Neither a trained model nor one favorable demo proves broad superiority over established compilers.

Use held-out circuit seeds and topologies, equal candidate counts for learned/random/heuristic search, and a graph-feature ablation. Report external compilers separately with measured time, configuration, and verification. A single baseline call is not automatically budget-equivalent to multiple QLOOM candidates. Publish ties, regressions, failed verification, and timeout outcomes. Keep model-training cost separate from inference/search cost.

Logical equivalence is an operator/channel property. Comparing only the all-zero input, noisy distributions, or output fidelity is insufficient. Account for initial/final placement, classical bit destinations, and global phase. Numerical synthesis can be equivalent up to global phase; record and, when strict phase preservation is required, restore that phase before declaring success. Unsupported control flow, resets, unbound parameters, and intermediate measurements must be rejected explicitly unless a sound verification path is implemented.

BQSKit has a Berkeley Lab three-clause BSD-style license **with an additional enhancements clause**, so retain and link its [full license text](https://github.com/BQSKit/bqskit/blob/main/LICENSE) rather than silently replacing it with a generic BSD label. Dependencies remain separately licensed. QLOOM's license cannot relicense them. Cite the algorithms and tools actually used; no dependency's results or branding imply endorsement.

## Adapter feasibility observed locally

The CPU environment imported Qiskit 2.6.1, Aer 0.18.0, QMAP 3.11.0, MQT Core 3.11.0, and BQSKit 1.2.1 on Python 3.12. All six baseline entry points executed on a three-qubit routing example. Qiskit levels 0–3 preserved the full operator to floating-point precision. QMAP and BQSKit produced the same logical operator up to a recorded global phase, which requires explicit restoration before a strict equivalence claim.

Independent scratch checks constructed permutation matrices directly and tested 27 Qiskit/QMAP compilations over three seeds and widths three through five on line devices. Every phase-aligned maximum matrix residual was below 1.2e-15. This caught an MQT Core 3.11 layout-conversion issue: its Qiskit layout helper can compose final placement against dictionary insertion order instead of physical wire order. The adapter composes initial and final placements using physical circuit wires, and independent verification remains mandatory. BQSKit required permission to open its local worker sockets in the execution sandbox; the scoped smoke test succeeded after that permission. These small checks establish adapter feasibility, not comparative benchmark superiority.

Further sparse-input checks exposed QMAP's unconditional idle-wire stripping in its [Mapper constructor](https://github.com/munich-quantum-toolkit/qmap/blob/main/src/sc/Mapper.cpp): logical labels can become compacted when unused source wires are removed. The adapter adds explicit zero-angle U identities only to otherwise untouched source wires, keeping the original wire labels. It removes only exact identity output operations and records both the inserted wire markers and the removal count. This handling preserves arbitrary states on idle logical wires rather than assuming they begin in zero. Sixty-two seeded sparse cases across widths four through six and line/ring/star/grid devices passed phase-aligned full-matrix checks below 4.1e-16; the original two failed mirror instances also passed the independent strict verifier after explicit phase restoration. Four empty-circuit widths were checked separately.

The default QMAP dynamic-placement policy produced a separate verification rejection on the five-qubit Fourier/ring instance. Its input conversion verified, but its native returned initial/final mapping did not explain the compiled operator; disabling postmapping optimization did not resolve that discrepancy. Static and identity placement policies passed that instance. No reference-dependent permutation correction is used. Any reported dynamic-policy failure remains a failure unless a documented, consistently evaluated baseline configuration replaces it.

The retained default is QMAP 3.11's **dynamic** placement for every benchmark instance. Reproduce the rejected case using `generate("qft", 5, 10402, layers=2)`, set `qc.global_phase = 0.173 * 5`, and call `compile_baseline(qc, make_device("ring", 5), "qmap", 0)`. The returned logical-to-physical mappings are initial `[0, 1, 4, 2, 3]` and final `[3, 2, 4, 1, 0]`. Restoring only global phase still leaves a maximum matrix residual of about `0.35355339`, far above `1e-8`; therefore the candidate is rejected. QMAP exposes no random seed for this policy, so its three repeated compiler-seed rows represent the same deterministic failure. The alternate static/identity-layout checks are diagnostic evidence only, not substituted benchmark successes.

Optional real-compiler regression tests run with `QLOOM_TEST_OPTIONAL_BASELINES=1 pytest tests/test_baselines.py`. They check sparse original wire labels, arbitrary states on idle wires, empty full-width unitaries, and isolated-worker timeout cleanup. They skip in the ordinary core-only test run.

## Diagram assistance investigated

[PaperVizAgent](https://github.com/google-research/papervizagent), formerly PaperBanana, provides a reference-driven illustration pipeline. Its documented execution expects configured model-provider APIs and its [requirements](https://github.com/google-research/papervizagent/blob/main/requirements.txt) include hosted-provider SDKs. Under the no-separately-billed-API constraint, an editable native diagram plus plots generated directly from experiment JSON is the feasible default. The project was investigated; no PaperVizAgent-generated figure or result is claimed.
